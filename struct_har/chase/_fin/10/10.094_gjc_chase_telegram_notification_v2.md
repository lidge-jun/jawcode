## Chase Scope

This card tracks Telegram as a remote observation and control surface rather than a final-message notifier. The upstream cluster adds `/verbose` and `/lean` activity modes, safe reasoning summaries, directed-replay provenance, per-client ask capabilities, shared notification service ownership, topic templates, child-session suppression, polling hygiene, stale-daemon reload, and selection acknowledgements.

JWC already has a split notification architecture with inbound routing, callback acknowledgement, redaction, rich turn delivery, threaded lifecycle, and daemon ownership. The chase must determine which upstream guarantees are already equivalent and which need adaptation to JWC's protocol-v1 frames and modular daemon pipeline.

## Worktree Verification

JWC has `/verbose`, `/lean`, and `/verbosity` parsing in `notifications/config-command-parser.ts`; top-level-only startup via `taskDepth` in `session-lifecycle.ts`; callback acknowledgement in the daemon inbound pipeline; ordered HTML turn delivery in `telegram-turn-delivery.ts`; and dedicated daemon owner, poll, topic/thread, pairing, redaction, and media modules. This is a strong partial implementation, including work from the existing 10.074 card family.

JWC's notification protocol remains version 1 with `turn_stream` text frames and no explicit tool-activity, reasoning-summary, replay-provenance, or client-capability fields. The targeted sweep does not establish upstream-equivalent directed replay identity, per-client ask-control negotiation, topic-name templates, or stale-generation reload semantics. Poll retry/backoff and malformed input tests exist, but the upstream poll-health classification and single shared CLI/command service need a module-by-module equivalence decision.
